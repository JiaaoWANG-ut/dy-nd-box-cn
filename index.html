Please read first
  • Rates and ΔG‡ are apparent committed-transition values and depend on the filter window W and the cutoff rc (see the sensitivity analysis in 4.4); the relative order of the three steps is stable, while absolute values shift by a few kJ/mol.
  • The pKa “equivalents” assume pKw = 14 and treat the OH− in the box as an ideal solution (single-molecule concentration c1 = 0.422 M solution / 0.359 M graphite); in reality the concentration is high and ion pairs form, far from infinite dilution.

1 Summary

Every number is read directly from the CSV files of the sections below

  • (a) REE–O coordination (300 K, box PBC): with DPA4C-Plus on graphite, Dy/Nd REE–O(total) = 8.09/8.09 (water 6.59/6.75, OH 1.50/1.34); in solution Dy = 7.79 (water 5.77, OH 2.02) and Nd = 8.30 (water 7.83, OH 0.48). With DPA4-Pro, MACE-POLAR-1-M and UMA in solution, REE–O(OH) = 2.99–3.00, i.e. all 3 OH stay coordinated, with correspondingly less water and a lower total CN (Dy 6.53–7.06, Nd 7.21–8.00; UMA graphite Dy/Nd = 6.76/7.20). The cutoff is the first minimum of the total REE–O g(r) (3.11–3.43 Å).
  • (b) OH-centred coordination: the first minimum of the O–X(total) g(r) (1.43–2.13 Å) lies before the O–REE peak (2.17–2.31 Å), so with the “strict first minimum” O–REE is always 0 and only hydrogen-bond donor H are counted (DPA4C-Plus also has a shared-proton peak at 1.23–1.25 Å). We therefore also report a “shell including the REE” cutoff (the minimum after the O–REE peak, 2.25–2.51 Å): for DPA4-Pro, MACE and UMA, O–X(total) = 3.08–3.48, i.e. O–REE 0.96–0.99 + O–H(water) 2.08–2.42 (≈ 1 REE + just over 2 water H); for DPA4C-Plus, O–X(total) = 2.95–3.22 is similar but the composition differs: O–REE is only 0.24–0.57 (most OH are not on the REE) and O–H(water) is larger (2.55–2.78). For DPA4C-Plus Nd on graphite the shell minimum barely exists (peak-to-valley drop 0.7%), and for DPA4C-Plus Nd in solution the shell peak is actually O···O(water) (O–X = 4.74, of which O–REE 0.15, including ≈ 1.0 water O); these two entries are diagnostic only.
  • Three-step apparent activation energies (Eyring, 300 K): graphite DPA4C-Plus has the best statistics (479–2229 committed events per direction). Forward hydrolysis (Ln gains OH−, (k−1)→k), steps 1/2/3: Dy 8.7/9.9/12.6, Nd 7.7/8.9/11.6 kJ/mol; reverse (Ln loses OH−, k→(k−1)): Dy 12.2/9.9/8.2, Nd 10.5/8.3/7.3 kJ/mol. Step 1 is the fastest forward and the slowest in reverse; step 3 is the opposite. Nd is 0.9–1.7 kJ/mol lower than Dy. ≥ 90% of the events are proton-transfer-like (the OH oxygen stays on Ln and only an H is exchanged).
  • Temperature dependence (solution, 300–500 K): only DPA4C-Plus (plus MACE Nd step 3) has enough events for Arrhenius fits; the apparent Ea with fit quality “good/fair” = −11.8 … 19.2 kJ/mol, with wide 95% CIs. Negative Ea appears in the OH-loss direction (e.g. Nd 2→1: −6.35 [−10.35, −2.43]), consistent with a van 't Hoff ΔH°d < 0 (Nd step 1: −23.25 kJ/mol): heating makes OH more likely to stay on Ln. These are composite apparent rate constants, not barriers of single elementary steps; with DPA4-Pro, MACE and UMA, OH almost never leaves Ln in solution, so they only give lower bounds on ΔG‡.
  • Comparison with Shiery 2021: the paper has thermodynamics only (pKa, ΔG, 298 K). The SI first-hydrolysis PMF rises monotonically (end point Nd 47.2, Dy 51.9 kJ/mol ≈ Table 1 ΔG1 47.4/51.9) with no barrier, so activation energies cannot be compared with the paper directly. The DPA4C-Plus pKa equivalents (pKw = 14) of all three steps fall within 12.7–14.7, with almost no stepwise separation (paper: Nd 8.3/12.9/17.8, Dy 9.1/16.1/21.2): pKa1 is higher than in the paper (ΔΔG1 = 20.8–36.7 kJ/mol) and pKa3 is lower (ΔΔG3 = −42.0 … −18.4 kJ/mol; other potentials, step 3: −50.0 … −33.4 kJ/mol). With the other potentials OH hardly ever leaves Ln in solution, so steps 1 and 2 are mostly “undetermined” or bounds only.
  • How to read Table 2: for Nd, Kragten 1984 (ref 99) lists 8.1/16.2/24.3 = 8.1 × n, which looks like cumulative constants −log*βn; the experimental medians for steps 2 and 3 are also numerically closer to cumulative values. The paper's MAD treats them as stepwise pKa (recomputing the MAD from Table 2, 38/39 agree with Table 1, and 6 entries have no experimental value). Section 6 gives both readings.

2.2 Coordination-number definitions (tasks a, b)

  • O species: every H is assigned to its nearest O by minimum-image distance; an O with 2 H = water, 1 H = OH−, 0 or ≥ 3 = other (O2−/H3O+). The assignment is redone in every frame, so OH and water swap identities after a proton transfer. REE–O(other): 0 in the graphite system; in solution (non-collapsed trajectories) |REE–O(total) − water − OH| ≤ 0.002, which is negligible, so REE–O(total) ≈ water + OH.
  • (a) REE-centred: cutoff rc = the minimum after the first peak of the total REE–O g(r) (g(r) grid 0.02 Å, 5-point smoothing). REE–O(water), REE–O(OH) and REE–O(total) are all counted directly with this single rc.
  • (b) OH-centred: centres = the O of every OH in each frame; X = all atoms except the central O itself and its own H (REE, water O/H, O/H of the other OH, and C in the graphite system). Reported: O–REE, O–H(water) and O–X(total).
  • Sampling and errors: graphite uses one frame per 1 ps from t ≥ 10 ps, with the between-trajectory standard error (weighted by frame count); solution uses the per-frame counts of md/dense-gr-cn (t ≥ 10 ps, every 2 fs, 70,001 frames), with 5-block standard errors.

2.3 What to do when the OH solvation shell is unclear

The O–X(total) g(r) of OH− is made of several overlapping peaks: water H donating hydrogen bonds to the OH (1.6–1.8 Å; DPA4C-Plus also has a shared proton at 1.23–1.25 Å), the REE (2.2–2.3 Å) and water O (2.4–2.7 Å). The “first minimum of the total g(r)” is therefore not unique. Our approach:

  1. Strict cutoff (the requested definition): the first minimum after the first real peak of O–X(total) (g > 0.5 followed by a drop of ≥ 10%). It always lies before the O–REE peak, so only hydrogen-bonded H are counted and O–REE ≈ 0.
  2. Shell cutoff: starting from the O–REE g(r) peak (the maximum within 1.9–2.8 Å), take the next minimum of O–X(total), so that the REE is included.
  3. Clarity: a peak-to-valley drop 1 − gmin/gpeak ≥ 10% counts as “clear”; otherwise the result is diagnostic only.
  4. Remainder check: inside the shell, X − REE − H(water) should be ≈ 0; if it is clearly > 0, the shell cutoff has already taken in water O.
  5. Reciprocal reference: REE–O(OH)/3 does not depend on any OH-centred cutoff (it uses the REE–O rc) and can be compared with the shell O–REE.

2.4 Collapse screening

Some UMA trajectories collapse (the potential energy plunges and H/O are pulled onto Ln). Criteria: (i) energy: the block average over every 50 samples (1 ps) falls 25 eV (graphite) / 15 eV (solution) below the minimum of the preceding 5 blocks, with the onset refined at −10 eV; (ii) geometry: Ln–O < 1.85 Å or Ln–H < 1.90 Å for 10 consecutive samples. Trajectories are cut 1 ps before the collapse; if ≥ 20 ps remain they are marked “truncated” and kept, otherwise “excluded”. 91 trajectories were excluded in total (all 5 UMA Dy solution trajectories). Graphite CN/g(r) and all kinetics use only the usable time span; solution CN/g(r) come from md/dense-gr-cn, which gives no CN for collapsed trajectories; hence UMA-s-1.2.1 Nd 500 K has kinetics results only (from the first 66 ps).

4 Three-step apparent activation energies

Committed-transition rates → Eyring ΔG‡ (300 K) and Arrhenius Ea (solution 300–500 K)

4.1 Step mapping and method

Paper step kPaper: acid dissociation (298 K; step 1 constrained AIMD, steps 2 and 3 DFT cluster + CPCM)MD forward: Ln gains OH−MD reverse: Ln loses OH−
1Ln3+ + H2O → LnOH2+ + H+n: 0 → 1n: 1 → 0
2LnOH2+ + H2O → Ln(OH)2+ + H+n: 1 → 2n: 2 → 1
3Ln(OH)2+ + H2O → Ln(OH)3 + H+n: 2 → 3n: 3 → 2

States and events

  • n(t) = number of OH− oxygens within rc of Ln (OH = an O with exactly 1 nearest H), capped at 3, one frame every 20 fs.
  • rc = the minimum after the first peak of the Ln–O(OH) radial density (3.03–3.47 Å, see the table below).
  • Committed transitions: a centred mode filter of width W = 0.5 ps, after which segments shorter than W/2 are absorbed; this removes back-and-forth jumps caused by O–H vibration.
  • Rates are counted from t ≥ 10 ps, occupancies from t ≥ 10 ps (solution) / 50 ps (graphite).
  • In graphite DPA4C-Plus, 90–97% of the events are “proton-transfer-like”: the O stays on Ln and only an H is exchanged (Ln–OH2 + OH− ⇌ Ln–OH + H2O). This is exactly the acid-dissociation step of the paper, except that the proton acceptor is OH− rather than water.

Rates and apparent barriers

k = N / Torigin
ΔG‡ = RT ln(kBT / hk)
ln k = ln A − Ea/RT; ln(k/T) = ln(kB/h) + ΔS‡/R − ΔH‡/RT
  • N = number of transitions, Torigin = total dwell time in the origin state. 95% CI = envelope of a bootstrap (2000 resamples; graphite resampled by trajectory, solution, with only 1 trajectory per T, resampled in 10 ps blocks) and the Garwood Poisson interval. When N is very small the CI can extend to ΔG‡ < 0 or ∞ (resamples with 0 events); such values only indicate an order of magnitude.
  • For N = 0 a lower bound is given by inserting the Poisson upper-limit rate 3.689/Torigin into Eyring.
  • Arrhenius fits are weighted by N. Fit quality: good = ≥ 4 T, ≥ 20 events and ≥ 3 at every T; fair = ≥ 3 T and ≥ 8 events; otherwise poor; “none” if fewer than 2 T have events.

7 Limitations

Read this before interpreting the numbers

  • Apparent quantities: committed rates depend on the filter window and the cutoff; e.g. ΔG‡ of graphite DPA4C-Plus Dy 3→2 is 6.2 / 10.1 kJ/mol at W = 0.2 / 2.0 ps. The relative order of the three steps (forward 1 < 2 < 3, reverse 1 > 2 > 3) holds in all 18 “setting × metal” combinations (section 4.4) and is more reliable than the absolute values.
  • Few statistics in solution: there is only one 150 ps trajectory per temperature; DPA4C-Plus at 300 K has only 0–12 events per direction, and the 95% CIs of Ea are wide (see section 4.3).
  • Assumptions behind the pKa equivalents: pKw = 14, ideal solution, and a standard-state conversion with c1 = 1 molecule per box = 0.42 M (solution) / 0.36 M (graphite); the free OH− concentration (4 − n)c1 reaches about 1 M, with no activity or ion-pair corrections; the difference between 300 K and 298.15 K is neglected.
  • Starting-configuration memory: the graphite DPA4C-Plus occupancies converge only after ~90 ps (section 5.4).
  • UMA collapse: all UMA Dy solution trajectories are excluded; graphite UMA: Dy 79 excluded, 116 truncated, median usable length 44 ps; Nd 7 excluded, 116 truncated, median usable length 125 ps. UMA results are for reference only.
  • The potentials differ qualitatively: with DPA4-Pro, MACE and UMA the 3 OH stay on Ln almost all the time (300 K REE–O(OH) = 2.89–3.00), whereas with DPA4C-Plus OH leaves frequently; which is closer to reality needs independent validation by AIMD or experimental structures.
  • Comparability with the paper: the paper's pKa1 is the PMF end point of constrained AIMD in a small box (64 waters, 15 ps per point), and its pKa2 and pKa3 are relative free energies of fixed-CN clusters (Nd 9, Dy 8) + CPCM; the paper has only 298 K thermodynamics (and the reading of the step 2 and 3 experimental values is ambiguous, section 6.4), with no barriers. Our Ea cannot be compared with the paper directly; only its magnitude can be set against the paper's ΔG.
  • OH solvation shell: for graphite DPA4C-Plus Nd the O–X(total) shell minimum is unclear (section 3.2), so the shell O–X(total) of these entries should not be used for conclusions; use O–REE(shell) and REE–O(OH)/3 instead.
