Reactions and definitions

Step-n acid dissociation (n = 1, 2, 3; spectator waters omitted):

n = 1:  Ln3+(H2O) ⇌ Ln(OH)12+ + H+
n = 2:  Ln(OH)12+(H2O) ⇌ Ln(OH)2+ + H+
n = 3:  Ln(OH)2+(H2O) ⇌ Ln(OH)3 + H+
Ka,n= [Ln(OH)n(3−n)+][H+] [Ln(OH)n−1(4−n)+] ,pKa,n=−log10Ka,n ,ΔGn=RTln10·pKa,n

Methods

Shiery 2021, calculated (Table 1, 298.15 K)

  • pKa1: constrained AIMD (Bluemoon, CP2K/PBE, 64 H2O + 3 Cl−). The r(O–H) of a coordinated water is the reaction coordinate, with 9 constraint points from 0.99 to 1.40 Å and 15 ps NVT each; integrating the mean constraint force ⟨f⟩ gives the PMF, whose end-point free energy is converted to pKa1 (error as given in Table 1).
A(r)=−∫r0r ⟨f⟩r′dr′ ,pKa,1= A(rend)−A(r0)RTln10
  • pKa2, pKa3: reaction free energies of the three steps from M06 clusters (explicit first-shell water and OH) + CPCM implicit solvent, taken relative to pKa1; the cluster coordination number is fixed at 9 for Nd and 8 for Dy.
pKa,n=pKa,1+ ΔGnM06/CPCM−ΔG1M06/CPCMRTln10 ,n=2,3

Shiery 2021, experiment (Table 2)

  • Compilation of literature values; the median is listed. Note: the step-2 and step-3 values are numerically closer to cumulative constants −log*βn (Ln3+ + n H2O ⇌ Ln(OH)n(3−n)+ + n H+), whereas the paper uses them as stepwise constants.

This work: MD occupancy → pKa equivalent (300 K)

  • Systems: Ln3+ + 3 OH− + water (solution: 128 H2O; graphite/water interface: 4 initial OH configurations × 50 trajectories), 3D-periodic box.
  • State n = number of OH− in the first shell of Ln (OH = an O with exactly one nearest H; cutoff rc = first minimum of the Ln–O(OH) radial density, 3.03–3.47 Å), sampled every 20 fs.
  • Pn: equilibrium probability of state n (t ≥ 10 ps for solution, t ≥ 50 ps for graphite); there are (4 − n) unbound OH−.
  • c1: concentration of one molecule in the box, 0.4224 M (solution) and 0.3594 M (graphite); c° = 1 M. Ideal solution, no activity corrections.

What MD observes directly is OH− dissociation:

Ln(OH)n(3−n)+ ⇌ Ln(OH)n−1(4−n)+ + OH−
Kd,n= [Ln(OH)n−1][OH−][Ln(OH)n] =Pn−1Pn· (4−n)c1c° ,c1=1NAV

Adding water autoionization H2O ⇌ H+ + OH− (Kw, pKw = 14) gives the acid dissociation above:

Ka,n=KwKd,n ⇒ pKa,n=pKw+log10Kd,n =14+log10[Pn−1Pn(4−n)c1]

If only state n occurs (state n − 1 never seen), assuming the unseen state is not more populated than the seen one and setting Pn−1 = Pn gives an upper bound:

pKa,n≤14+log10[(4−n)c1] (Pn−1:=Pn)
  • 95% CI: 2000 bootstrap resamples (graphite: by trajectory, stratified by initial configuration; solution: 10 ps blocks).
  • Conversion: ΔG = RT ln10 · pKa; RT ln10 = 5.708 kJ/mol at 298.15 K and 5.743 kJ/mol at 300 K.
